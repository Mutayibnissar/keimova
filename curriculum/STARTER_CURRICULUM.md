# Keimova starter curriculum: evidence map and delivery blueprint

**Status:** Starter blueprint for curriculum production, not a claim that complete courses have been peer-reviewed.  
**Review date:** 15 October 2026  
**Owner:** Keimova curriculum team

## Evidence rules

- Treat Google Scholar as a discovery index, not a guarantee that a result is peer-reviewed or reliable.
- Read the original publication or official source before using a claim. Never cite only a search snippet or an AI summary.
- For research-based claims, record author(s), title, publication, year, DOI/canonical URL, source type, relevant section/page, access date and the precise claim supported.
- Prefer systematic reviews, peer-reviewed original studies, academic textbooks, official standards and reputable professional bodies when appropriate to the lesson.
- Label examples and simulated numbers as illustrative. Never present generated case data as actual market research.
- Recheck fast-changing material before publishing and retain a dated revision history.
- Respect source licences and copyright. Linking to a source does not permit copying its text, figures or course materials.

## Starter course maps

### 1. Business Administration Essentials
**Audience:** Beginner  
**Outcomes:** Explain core organisational concepts; analyse an organisation's external environment; make a structured recommendation using evidence and trade-offs.  
**Sequence:**
1. Organisations, stakeholders and value creation
2. External environment and industry context
3. Organisational structures and coordination
4. Strategy choices and decision trade-offs
5. Applied case: analyse a fictional small business
**Assessment:** Short case analysis graded on problem framing, evidence, options, reasoning and clarity.  
**Starter source:** OpenStax, *Principles of Management*, section 4.1, “The Organization's External Environment”: https://openstax.org/books/principles-management/pages/4-1-the-organizations-external-environment  
**Research task:** Use Google Scholar to find recent peer-reviewed research on environmental scanning, strategic adaptation and small-business decision-making. Verify each selected paper at the publisher or repository.

### 2. Marketing Strategy & Growth
**Audience:** Beginner  
**Outcomes:** Describe a target customer; develop a positioning statement; connect a campaign objective to measurable indicators; distinguish assumptions from observed data.  
**Sequence:**
1. Customer problem and market context
2. Segmentation, targeting and positioning
3. Value proposition and marketing mix
4. Channel planning and budget assumptions
5. Measurement, experimentation and learning
**Assessment:** Campaign brief with target segment, positioning, channel rationale, KPI definitions and an explicit assumptions section.  
**Starter source:** American Marketing Association, “The Definition of Marketing”: https://www.ama.org/the-definition-of-marketing-what-is-marketing/  
**Research task:** Search scholarly work on positioning, customer behaviour, marketing measurement and experimental design. Note study context and limits before generalising findings.

### 3. Human Resource Management & People Analytics
**Audience:** Beginner to intermediate  
**Outcomes:** Map the employee lifecycle; design job-relevant structured interview criteria; interpret a simple workforce metric without overstating causation.  
**Sequence:**
1. HRM and organisational outcomes
2. Workforce planning and recruitment
3. Selection, structured interviews and fairness
4. Performance, learning and employee experience
5. People metrics, privacy and responsible interpretation
**Assessment:** A fictional recruitment scorecard plus a short workforce-data interpretation memo. Use fictional or properly de-identified data only.  
**Starter source:** CIPD, “Impact of human resource management: An evidence review” (published 22 August 2025): https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/evidence-reviews/impact-hr-management/  
**Additional source:** CIPD, “Effective people managers: Evidence review” (published 5 July 2023): https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/evidence-reviews/people-managers/  
**Research task:** Review the evidence summaries and, where accessible, the scientific summaries/original studies. Explain the difference between association, plausible mechanism and demonstrated causal effect.

### 4. AI Tools for Business Workflows
**Audience:** Beginner  
**Outcomes:** Select a suitable AI-assisted workflow; write a testable prompt; verify output against source material; identify privacy, security, reliability and bias risks.  
**Sequence:**
1. AI capabilities and limitations
2. Task selection and human oversight
3. Prompt design and output evaluation
4. Data privacy and safe input handling
5. Risk mapping, testing and documentation
**Assessment:** A workflow specification with sample input/output, evaluation rubric, failure cases and human-review checkpoints. Do not submit confidential or personal data to public tools.  
**Starter source:** NIST, *Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework (AI RMF 1.0)*, NIST AI 100-1 (2023): https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1  
**Current-status check:** NIST indicates the framework is being updated; check the official page before each course revision: https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/  
**Research task:** Use scholarly literature and official guidance to compare evaluation methods and risks for the specific workflow, rather than presenting a single framework as universally sufficient.

### 5. Business Analytics Foundations
**Audience:** Beginner  
**Outcomes:** Define a business question; clean a small dataset; calculate and explain descriptive measures; distinguish descriptive findings from causal claims.  
**Sequence:**
1. Turning a vague request into an answerable question
2. Data types, quality and missing values
3. Descriptive statistics and spreadsheet checks
4. SQL query concepts and reproducibility
5. Communicating findings, uncertainty and limitations
**Assessment:** A short analysis using a clearly labelled sample dataset, a reproducible calculation/query and a one-page findings note.  
**Starter source:** OpenStax open textbook catalogue: https://openstax.org/subjects/business  
**Research task:** Add current official documentation for the chosen spreadsheet/SQL tool and scholarly sources for statistical interpretation, sampling and common analytical pitfalls. Do not use this broad catalogue link as a substitute for lesson-specific references.

## Standard lesson template

Every published lesson should contain:
1. Learning outcomes written with observable verbs.
2. A concise explanation in original wording.
3. A worked example with assumptions identified.
4. A practical task and expected submission.
5. A short knowledge check with answer explanations.
6. “Evidence and further reading” with lesson-level references.
7. Source quality notes and known limitations.
8. A last-reviewed date and content version.

## Assessment quality checklist

- Each question maps to a stated learning outcome.
- Distractors in MCQs are plausible and feedback explains why an answer is right or wrong.
- Case studies make fictional data and assumptions explicit.
- Rubrics describe observable performance, not vague traits.
- Accessibility, language clarity and mobile usability are checked.
- The assessment does not imply professional certification, employment or guaranteed income.

## Next production step
