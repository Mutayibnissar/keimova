# Keimova content evidence and source policy

Keimova is intended to teach practical business, management, marketing, HR, finance, analytics, AI and career skills using transparent, checkable references. The current website is still a prototype: its catalogue descriptions and learning objectives are editorial outlines, not a fully researched or peer-reviewed curriculum. The source links help learners investigate a topic; they do not mean every statement has already been independently validated.

## Source hierarchy

1. **Peer-reviewed research and scholarly literature**
   - [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/) is a discovery tool for papers, books, theses, preprints, reports and other scholarly literature. Search by topic, inspect the original publisher/repository record, and prefer recent systematic reviews or high-quality primary research when appropriate.
   - [Google Scholar search help](https://scholar.google.co.in/intl/en/scholar/help.html) explains sorting by date, locating full text and following citations.
   - Scholar results are not all peer-reviewed, current, open access or equally reliable. Check publication type, date, methods, author affiliations, conflicts and citations before using a result as evidence.

2. **Open educational materials**
   - [OpenStax higher education](https://openstax.org/higher-education) provides educator-authored, peer-reviewed, openly licensed textbooks and supporting materials.
   - [OpenLearn](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/) provides free learning materials from The Open University.

3. **Professional and standards bodies**
   - [American Marketing Association](https://www.ama.org/) for marketing concepts and professional resources.
   - [CIPD knowledge hub](https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/) for HR and people-practice resources.
   - [NIST AI Risk Management Framework](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/) for responsible AI risk concepts and practices.

## Required workflow before publishing a course

- Define measurable learning outcomes and the intended learner level.
- Build an evidence table with: claim/concept, source title, authors/organisation, publication date, source type, DOI or canonical URL, access date, relevant page/section, and a short note on how the source supports the lesson.
- Prioritise original publications, systematic reviews, academic textbooks, government/standards documents and reputable professional bodies. Use news, blogs and vendor material only when fit for purpose and clearly labelled.
- Verify factual claims against the original source rather than a search snippet or AI-generated summary.
- Separate established evidence, professional guidance, illustrative examples and opinions. Mark uncertain or contested claims.
- For quantitative claims, record the population, geography, period, units, assumptions and calculation. Do not present simulated outputs as real-world data.
- Create original explanations and exercises; quote only what is necessary, cite it, and respect copyright and licence terms. A link to a resource does not grant permission to reproduce it.
- Date-stamp reviews and schedule re-checks for fast-changing topics such as AI, technology, laws and market data.
- Have a subject-matter reviewer check high-stakes or specialist material before describing it as expert-reviewed.

## How source links currently work

Learning-item modals provide a topic-specific Google Scholar search plus a relevant open-learning, professional or standards resource where available. These are **discovery links**, not a claim that a course has completed formal evidence review. Keimova does not scrape Google Scholar; its search page is opened for the learner to inspect. Google Scholar does not provide a general public search API for unrestricted bulk harvesting, so automated scraping should not be used.

## Prototype status

The current catalogue contains sample course descriptions and learning objectives. It is not yet a full LMS or a verified academic course library. Before public launch, each course should gain a curated bibliography, lesson-level citations, reviewed assessments, clear licences, version/review dates and accessible source links. No certification, job, income or academic-credit outcome should be promised unless separately established.
